How this is worked out

Totals come from the half-hour history, which is kept for good, so these trends cover everything recorded. Each half hour is priced like the battery payback: your Octopus prices where they were fetched, otherwise the prices in Edit costs. Grid cost is unit prices only; the standing charge isn't included. Projections assume the rest of the period goes like the average of the last 14 recorded days, so they follow the season but swing with the weather; the paler part of each bar is the projected rest of the current week or month.

Weather comes from Open-Meteo (free, no account) for the latitude and longitude you give, rounded to about a kilometre; nothing else is sent. It is kept for good, so solar output can be set against the sunshine it had (solar yield, kWh per kWh/m² of sunshine) and capacity against temperature. Peaks are the highest single reading in a minute. The Solarbank 4's Modbus map has no battery temperature or per-cell voltages, so outdoor temperature and the Smart Meter's grid voltage stand in for them.

The battery doesn't report its state of health over Modbus, so capacity is measured: on each long discharge (at least 20 points of battery level with no charging in between), the energy discharged is divided by the share of the battery level it used. Battery levels come in whole percent, so a single week's figure can move a few percent either way, and it reads a little under the rated capacity because of the battery's own losses. A slow fall over months is the thing to watch.

            Quick override

            Charge, hold or discharge for a while without making a schedule. It ends by itself and works even when control is off.

            Anker app mode

            The battery uses this mode whenever the dashboard isn't charging, holding or discharging it. Now: unknown

            Battery reserve and limits

            The battery's own limits, the same as in the Anker app. They apply all the time, solar charging included, whether or not dashboard control is on.

            For a longer battery life, stop at 80–90% most days (a full charge now and then is fine) and keep a 10–15% reserve. The reserve is the lowest the battery discharges to (the Anker app's discharge limit, 0–20%); dashboard schedules and holds respect it. The charge limit accepts 80–100%.

                Activity

                  Battery payback

                  Enter what you paid for the battery and your electricity prices to see how long it takes to pay for itself.

                  How this is worked out

                  Each kWh the battery discharges is valued at the price for that half hour, as grid electricity it saved you buying. Charging costs the price for that half hour when the energy came from the grid, and your export rate when it came from solar you could have sold. Charging while the grid supplies power counts as grid charging. Totals start when this dashboard began recording. If you enter the install date, the energy the battery moved before then (from its own lifetime totals) is added, valued at the average price per kWh recorded so far. The projection needs at least a week of savings, and is shown as a range because savings per day vary a lot with the seasons.
